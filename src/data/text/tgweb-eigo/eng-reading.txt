### Passage 1: The Rise of Remote Teams
Over the past decade, an increasing number of companies have built teams whose members never meet in person. Software makes it possible to share documents, hold video meetings and track progress from anywhere in the world. ( 1 ), businesses can hire the most suitable people regardless of where they live.

Remote teams, however, face particular challenges. Without casual conversations in corridors or at lunch, team members may find it hard to build trust. Misunderstandings can also arise more easily in written messages, which lack the tone of voice and facial expressions that help us ( 2 ) what others mean.

Successful remote teams tend to set clear rules about how and when to communicate. Some schedule short video calls simply to chat about non-work topics, which helps colleagues get to know each other. Managers also [ emphasise ] the results that employees achieve rather than the number of hours they appear to be online.

### Passage 2: Food Labels
Food labels provide information about the ingredients and nutritional content of products. In many countries, manufacturers are required by law to list the ingredients in order of weight, ( 1 ) the ingredient present in the largest amount comes first. Labels must also show common allergens, such as nuts and milk, so that people with allergies can avoid them.

Despite this information, many shoppers find labels difficult to use. The text is often small, and terms such as "low fat" or "natural" can be ( 2 ). A product described as low in fat, for example, may still contain a large amount of sugar.

To make labels easier to understand, some governments have introduced a system of coloured symbols on the front of packages. Green, amber and red symbols show at a glance whether a product is low, medium or high in fat, sugar and salt. Studies suggest that such symbols help shoppers make healthier choices, especially when they are in a hurry.

E: 直後に「脂肪が少ないと書かれた商品でも砂糖が多いことがある」という例があるので、「誤解を招く（misleading）」が入ります。

### Passage 3: Learning a Musical Instrument as an Adult
Many adults believe that it is too late to learn a musical instrument if they did not start as children. It is true that children often pick up new skills quickly. ( 1 ), adults have advantages of their own. They can understand music theory more easily, set clear goals and organise their practice time effectively.

The biggest challenge for adult learners is often finding time. Work and family responsibilities can make regular practice difficult. Teachers recommend short daily sessions rather than one long session at the weekend, since regular practice helps the brain ( 2 ) new movements.

Adult learners also tend to be harder on themselves. They may feel embarrassed when they make mistakes, especially in front of others. Teachers encourage them to see mistakes as a normal part of learning rather than a sign of failure. Many adults who take up an instrument say that the [ sense of achievement ] they feel when they master a new piece makes the effort worthwhile.

### Passage 4: Urban Trees
Trees play an important role in cities. They provide shade on hot days, absorb rainwater and improve air quality. Research has also shown that people living near green spaces tend to report lower levels of stress.

Planting trees in cities, ( 1 ), is not as simple as it might seem. Space is limited, and roots can damage pavements and underground pipes. Young trees also need regular watering during their first few years, which requires time and money.

For these reasons, city planners carefully choose which species to plant. Trees with deep roots are less likely to damage pavements, and species that can survive dry periods need less care. Some cities now [ involve ] local residents by asking them to water young trees near their homes. This not only reduces costs but also gives residents a stronger sense of ( 2 ) for their neighbourhood.

E: 前の段落で木の利点を述べ、この段落では「見かけほど簡単ではない」と反対の内容を述べているので however が入ります。

Q: Which word best fills blank ( 2 )?

### Passage 5: Customer Feedback
Many companies ask customers to rate their experience after a purchase or a phone call. This feedback can help businesses identify problems and improve their services. ( 1 ), if customers repeatedly complain that delivery takes too long, a company may decide to work with a different delivery firm.

However, collecting feedback is only the first step. Some companies gather large amounts of data but rarely act on it. Customers who notice that nothing changes may stop ( 2 ) feedback altogether, believing that their opinions are ignored.

The most effective companies show customers that their comments make a difference. Some send messages explaining what has been changed in response to suggestions. Others publish regular updates on their websites. By [ closing the loop ] in this way, companies encourage customers to keep sharing their views.
